# Elon Musk — Blind Reference Dossier

*A neutral, fact-rich reference compiled entirely from Elon Musk's prior public record: his own talks, posts, shareholder communications, company blog posts, SEC and court filings, biographies, and contemporaneous reporting. Figures vary across sources where noted.*

## Biography and Career Arc

Elon Reeve Musk was born June 28, 1971, in Pretoria, South Africa, to Maye Musk, a Canadian-born model and dietitian, and Errol Musk, a South African engineer and property developer. His parents divorced around 1979. A self-taught programmer, he reportedly sold a BASIC game called "Blastar" around age twelve. He emigrated to Canada in June 1989 using citizenship derived from his Canadian-born mother, attended Queen's University in Kingston, Ontario, then transferred to the University of Pennsylvania, earning a BA in physics and a BS in economics from Wharton (coursework finished 1995, degrees conferred 1997). He enrolled in a Stanford graduate program in 1995 and withdrew after roughly two days.

His first company, Zip2 (founded 1995 with brother Kimbal and Greg Kouri), was acquired by Compaq in 1999 for about $307 million; Musk netted roughly $22 million. He then founded X.com (1999), which merged with Confinity and was renamed PayPal. eBay acquired PayPal in a stock deal valued near $1.5 billion, closing October 3, 2002; as the largest shareholder, Musk received roughly $165 million (some accounts cite $175–180 million). He became a naturalized U.S. citizen in 2002.

Musk has fathered a large number of children across several partners — commonly cited as roughly fourteen, though the exact count is contested. His first child, Nevada, died of SIDS at about ten weeks in 2002. He became the world's richest person on January 7, 2021, at roughly $185–190 billion per Bloomberg, and in December 2024 became the first person reported to exceed $400 billion, peaking near $486 billion before retreating in early 2025 amid a Tesla decline. (A widely circulated "$285B" figure for the January 2021 milestone appears to be a transcription error.)

## SpaceX and Mars

Musk incorporated SpaceX on March 14, 2002, funding it with roughly $100 million of his own PayPal proceeds. The origin was "Mars Oasis," a 2001 concept to land a robotic greenhouse on Mars; after failed negotiations to buy refurbished Russian ICBMs, he concluded he should build cheaper rockets himself. His stated motivation is making humanity multiplanetary as a backup for consciousness and civilization, with low launch cost as the critical enabler.

Falcon 1 became, on September 28, 2008 (its fourth attempt, after three failures), the first privately developed liquid-fueled rocket to reach orbit. On May 25, 2012, Dragon became the first commercial spacecraft to berth with the ISS. On December 21, 2015, SpaceX achieved the first propulsive landing of an orbital-class booster, and on March 30, 2017, it reflew a recovered booster. Falcon Heavy first flew February 6, 2018, carrying Musk's Tesla Roadster toward solar orbit, with 27 Merlin engines and more than 5 million pounds of thrust.

On reusability, Musk has claimed a Falcon 9 costs roughly $60 million to build but only $200,000–300,000 to fuel, with the booster representing about 70% of launch cost; he has cited Falcon 9 costs near $2,500/kg to LEO. Starship/Super Heavy stands about 121 meters tall, with 33 Raptor methane/oxygen engines on the booster producing roughly 74 MN of liftoff thrust and a target payload of 100–150 tonnes to LEO. Musk has claimed an eventual cost per launch of roughly $10 million, then $2–3 million, with propellant near $900,000. Integrated test flights began with IFT-1 (April 20, 2023, destroyed) through IFT-5 (October 13, 2024), which achieved the first "chopsticks" tower catch of the booster.

Starlink had surpassed roughly 12 million subscribers by mid-2026 with about 10,400 satellites in orbit (planned expansion toward 42,000) and revenue rising from roughly $7.7 billion (2024) toward $11.4 billion (2025). On Mars, Musk's 2016 IAC talk ("Making Humans a Multiplanetary Species") proposed roughly 100 people per ship toward a self-sustaining city of about 1 million people, with a goal of cutting cost from roughly $10 billion per person to about $200,000. He has cited a need for roughly 1,000 Starships over about 20 years. His Mars timelines have slipped repeatedly: crewed missions were variously targeted for 2024, then 2026, then 2029 or 2031, with first uncrewed flights now nominally targeting the late-2020s.

SpaceX valuation rose from about $74 billion (early 2021) to roughly $350 billion (December 2024) and around $400 billion (mid-2025). The strongest counterarguments are scientific and economic: a 2024 feasibility analysis argued Starship's high dry mass yields a delta-v deficit preventing Earth return as configured; NASA radiation models predict mortality risk above 5% over a multi-year mission, though Mars advocate Robert Zubrin disputes the most alarmist neuron-damage studies. Critics including outlets such as Gizmodo have called the million-settler timeline unrealistic, and the FAA has repeatedly grounded both Falcon 9 and Starship pending mishap investigations — an active and stated tension between SpaceX and its regulator.

## Tesla, EVs, and Energy

Tesla was founded in July 2003 by Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning; Musk was not a founder but led the early-2004 Series A round (roughly $6.5 million total, of which he contributed about $6.35 million) and became chairman. A 2009 settlement granted five people, including Musk, the right to be called co-founders. He became CEO in October 2008 during a near-bankruptcy resolved by a roughly $40 million convertible round closing on Christmas Eve 2008.

The three Master Plans articulate Tesla's strategy. The 2006 "Secret Master Plan" laid out entering at the high-priced, low-volume end and moving down-market: build a sports car, use the proceeds for an affordable car, then an even more affordable one. "Master Plan, Part Deux" (2016) added solar-plus-storage, expansion across vehicle segments, fleet-learning autonomy "10X safer than manual," and idle-car robotaxi earnings. "Master Plan Part 3" (April 2023) framed a global decarbonization thesis requiring roughly 240 TWh of storage, 30 TW of renewable power, and about $10.4 trillion of investment.

Vehicles progressed from the Roadster (2008) to the Model S (production June 2012), Model X (2015), Model 3 (launched July 2017 amid "production hell"), Model Y (2020), and Cybertruck (unveiled November 2019, its armored glass shattering on stage; deliveries from November 2023). Deliveries grew from roughly 499,550 (2020) to about 1,808,581 (2023), before declining to roughly 1,789,226 (2024) — the first annual decline in over a decade. Tesla surpassed Toyota as the most valuable automaker in July 2020 and crossed a $1 trillion market cap in October 2021.

In energy, Tesla launched Powerwall and Powerpack (2015) and Megapack (2019); storage deployments grew from 6.5 GWh (2022) to 31.4 GWh (2024). Tesla acquired SolarCity, co-founded by Musk's cousins, for about $2.6 billion in 2016; shareholders alleged a conflicted bailout, but Delaware courts ruled for Musk (2022, affirmed 2023). Gigafactories opened in Nevada, Shanghai (2019), Berlin (2022), and Texas (2022). Battery Day (September 2020) unveiled the tabless 4680 cell with claimed targets of 54% more range, 56% lower cost per kWh, and a roughly $25,000 EV "within three years" — a timeline not met.

On autonomy, Autonomy Day (April 2019) projected roughly 1 million robotaxis on the road in 2020, which did not materialize; FSD pricing fluctuated between roughly $5,000 and $15,000. The Cybercab robotaxi was unveiled October 2024 (target under roughly $30,000), and the Optimus humanoid (first shown August 2021) targets a roughly $20,000–30,000 price at scale. The August 2018 "funding secured" tweet about taking Tesla private at $420 led to an SEC settlement requiring Musk and Tesla each to pay $20 million and Musk to step down as chairman for three years. His 2018 performance-based pay package (peak value around $56 billion) was voided by Delaware Chancellor Kathaleen McCormick in January 2024, with the Delaware Supreme Court reversing the rescission in December 2025.

The strongest counterarguments come from NHTSA, which forced a roughly 2-million-vehicle Autopilot recall in December 2023 and has open FSD investigations; from short sellers such as Jim Chanos and David Einhorn; from analysts citing repeatedly missed self-driving timelines; and from competitors — notably Waymo, which operates driverless robotaxis at scale while Tesla's remained limited.

## Artificial Intelligence, xAI, and Grok

Musk's AI alarm dates to August 2014, when he warned AI is "potentially more dangerous than nukes," and the MIT AeroAstro symposium that October, where he called AI "probably our biggest existential threat" and said "we are summoning the demon." He urged proactive regulation at the National Governors Association (2017) and SXSW (2018), and signed the Future of Life Institute's March 2023 "Pause Giant AI Experiments" letter calling for a six-month pause on systems more powerful than GPT-4. He publicly backed California's SB 1047 in 2024.

He co-founded OpenAI as a nonprofit (announced December 2015) with a pledged $1 billion. His contribution totals are disputed (OpenAI says under $45 million; litigation cites around $44 million). He left the board in February 2018, citing a Tesla conflict; OpenAI later said the deeper friction was his push to take control. After OpenAI created its capped-profit subsidiary (2019), Musk sued in February 2024, withdrew, then refiled federally (August 2024). In February 2025 a Musk-led consortium made an unsolicited roughly $97.4 billion bid for OpenAI's nonprofit assets, which Altman rejected; OpenAI countersued in April 2025, calling it a "sham bid."

Musk founded xAI (announced July 2023) with a mission to build "maximally truth-seeking" AI. Grok models progressed from Grok-1 (November 2023, later open-sourced as a 314-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts) through Grok-4 (July 2025). The Colossus supercomputer in Memphis came online around September 2024, built in 122 days with 100,000 NVIDIA H100 GPUs, later doubled to 200,000 with a stated goal of 1 million. xAI raised $6 billion Series B (May 2024, roughly $24 billion valuation) and $6 billion Series C (December 2024). On March 28, 2025, Musk announced xAI had acquired X in an all-stock deal valuing xAI at $80 billion and X at $33 billion. He has predicted AI smarter than any single human by around 2025–2026 and smarter than all humans combined by roughly 2029, while estimating a 10–20% chance AI "goes bad."

The central tension, stated as fact, is that Musk warned of AI as existential for a decade and then built a frontier lab racing to scale, justifying it as keeping a safety-focused builder at the frontier. Sam Altman has characterized Musk as operating "from a position of insecurity," and Yann LeCun has called Musk's AGI and robotaxi predictions "blatantly false." Grok's "anti-woke" framing has collided with its truth-seeking claim in incidents including a May 2025 "white genocide" output and a July 2025 antisemitic-output episode, both attributed by xAI to internal errors.

## Neuralink

Neuralink was founded in 2016 to build implantable brain-computer interfaces, with Musk's stated long-term goal of human "symbiosis with AI." The N1 implant carries 1,024 electrodes across 64 threads, placed by the R1 surgical robot. Public demos included the pig "Gertrude" (August 2020) and a macaque "Pager" playing MindPong (April 2021). The FDA approved human trials in May 2023, and the first human implant (Noland Arbaugh) occurred in late January 2024; roughly 85% of threads later retracted, mitigated via software. Funding reached a roughly $9 billion pre-money valuation by mid-2025. The strongest criticism, from the Physicians Committee for Responsible Medicine and PETA, alleges animal-welfare violations in earlier UC Davis experiments; Neuralink acknowledges some animal deaths but disputes the cruelty characterization.

## The Boring Company

The Boring Company originated from a December 2016 Musk tweet about traffic and aims to cut tunneling costs roughly tenfold via its "Prufrock" boring machine. The Las Vegas Convention Center Loop (a $48.7 million contract) opened in 2021, using human-driven Tesla vehicles; expansion plans claim up to 90,000 passengers per hour, a figure critics dispute. The Hyperloop Alpha white paper (August 2013) proposed vacuum-tube travel above 700 mph; the concept was later largely abandoned, with the test track dismantled in 2022. A Series C round (April 2022) raised $675 million at roughly $5.7 billion. Critics including transit expert Jarrett Walker call it a low-capacity car tunnel that underdelivers on its original automated, high-throughput vision.

## X (Twitter)

Musk disclosed a roughly 9.2% Twitter stake in early April 2022, offered $54.20 per share, and reached a roughly $44 billion deal that month. He attempted to terminate over spam-account disputes, was sued in Delaware, and closed the acquisition October 27, 2022. He fired top executives, cut the workforce from roughly 7,500 to about 1,500, introduced paid verification, and rebranded to "X" in July 2023. After he endorsed an antisemitic post in November 2023, major advertisers paused spending, and Fidelity later marked the company down to roughly $9.4 billion. Styling himself a "free-speech absolutist" who would make X the "digital town square," Musk faced criticism from the ADL and others that reduced moderation enabled hate speech, and that his December 2022 suspensions of journalists contradicted his stated absolutism. X was merged into xAI in March 2025.

## Management Philosophy and First Principles

Musk's signature method, articulated in his 2013 TED talk, is reasoning from "fundamental truths" rather than by analogy. His canonical example priced battery-pack materials at roughly $80/kWh against a prevailing market price near $600/kWh, motivating the Gigafactory. His five-step "algorithm" (per Walter Isaacson's 2023 biography) is: make requirements less dumb, delete the part or process, simplify and optimize, accelerate cycle time, and automate last. Related maxims include "the best part is no part" and the "idiot index" (a component's finished cost relative to its raw-material cost), both feeding a vertical-integration philosophy. He is known for an extreme "hardcore" work culture, including sleeping at the Fremont factory during Model 3 production. Critics note that first-principles material-cost math treats a floor as if it were achievable production cost, glossing over yields and capital expenditure.

## Politics, Free Speech, and Predictions

Once a self-described moderate and Obama-era voter, Musk shifted rightward, popularizing the phrase "woke mind virus." In 2024 he endorsed Donald Trump and spent over $250 million backing him, largely through America PAC. In 2025 he led the "Department of Government Efficiency" (DOGE), initially touting $2 trillion in cuts, later revised downward; independent reviewers (PolitiFact, NPR, and others) documented pervasive errors in DOGE's savings tracker, including an ICE contract listed as $8 billion that was actually $8 million. He departed the role around April–May 2025, followed by a public falling-out with Trump. He has also faced speech conflicts in Brazil (an X ban under Justice Alexandre de Moraes) and the EU (Digital Services Act proceedings), and warns that "a collapsing birth rate is the biggest danger civilization faces."

His prediction record shows a consistent pattern of bold but slipping timelines: Tesla full self-driving promised "next year" repeatedly from 2014 onward, 1 million robotaxis by 2020 (none delivered), a March 2020 forecast of "close to zero new" U.S. COVID cases by the end of April, a never-built NY–DC Hyperloop, and a Model 3 production target hit just past its self-set deadline. Several long-run goals — orbital rocket reusability, mass-market EVs, satellite internet — were eventually realized, even as the dates routinely slipped.
