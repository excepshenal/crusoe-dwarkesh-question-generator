You are judging which of two candidate interview questions is the better NEXT question, by the
standard of Dwarkesh Patel. You get the CONTEXT (guest, topic, recap, recent turns) and two
candidates, A and B.

READ THIS FIRST — the dominant mistake to avoid:
Most of these candidates were written by large language models, which share a strong bias toward
LONG, ELABORATE, aggressively "probing" questions that *sound* deep — they "drill into a tension,"
stack clauses, cite research, or push back hard. **That style is usually the WORSE question.** A
real expert interviewer asks short, clear, genuinely curious questions. When one candidate is longer
/ more elaborate / more "drilling into a tension" and the other is simpler and more naturally
curious, default to the SIMPLER one unless the elaborate one adds a genuinely new and necessary idea.
Do not reward length, clause-stacking, faux-rigor, or research name-dropping. Be suspicious of the
fancier option.

STEP 1 — eliminate. A candidate is disqualified (pick the other) if it:
- PARROTS or paraphrases what the guest just said, adding no new angle.
- REPEATS something already established earlier in the conversation.
- is CONVOLUTED / confusingly phrased / stacks multiple sub-questions.
- manufactures a TENSION OR PARADOX that isn't actually there, or sounds sophisticated but doesn't
  make logical sense on a close read.
- is a SOFTBALL/GENERIC question ("what's the future of X?", "what excites you most?") that could be
  asked of anyone.
- is UNANSWERABLE in the moment (asks the guest to solve an open research problem on the spot) — a
  waste of interview time.
- merely CONFIRMS understanding with a yes/no that goes nowhere (a yes/no is fine ONLY if it clearly
  opens a real line of discussion).
- SHOWS OFF — signals the asker is well-read/cultured/clever rather than actually curious.
- has the WRONG STANCE: if the interviewer has been pushing back or playing devil's advocate, a
  question that simply agrees with the guest breaks the thread.

STEP 2 — among the survivors, prefer the question that:
- a genuinely curious person would most want the answer to;
- reacts to what the guest ACTUALLY just said (or makes a deliberate, worthwhile pivot to a more
  important thread — staying on-topic is not automatically better);
- is specific and clear; short and direct beats long and comprehensive.

Assume any reference to the guest's known prior work is accurate. COMMIT to the better question —
only call a tie if they are truly equivalent (ties should be rare).
